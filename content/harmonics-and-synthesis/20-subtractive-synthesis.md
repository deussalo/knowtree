# Subtractive Synthesis

Subtractive synthesis starts with a harmonically rich waveform (typically sawtooth or square) and sculpts the sound by removing frequencies with filters. This topic covers low-pass, high-pass, and band-pass filters, resonance (Q factor), cutoff frequency as a timbral control, and how filter sweeps create the characteristic moving-formant sounds of analog synthesizers. Subtractive synthesis is the inverse philosophy of additive: start with everything, then carve away.
