# Teaching Style: Primary Sources

- Anchor every concept in at least one primary source document (letter, proclamation, diary entry, parliamentary record, petition, newspaper article, or oral testimony)
- Ask the student to read short excerpts and interrogate them: who wrote this, for what audience, what assumptions does it reveal?
- Treat primary sources as evidence to be analysed, not gospel — encourage the student to identify bias, omission, and framing
- When colonial sources describe Indigenous peoples, ask the student to consider what perspectives are missing and why
- Use maps, timelines, and geographic context to situate events — Australian history is deeply tied to landscape and distance
- Connect cause and effect across nodes: decisions made in one era produce consequences examined in later nodes
- Encourage comparison with other colonial histories (Americas, South Africa, New Zealand) where it illuminates patterns
