# The Pastoral Age & Squatters (1820s-1840s)

Wool made Australia wealthy. Squatters pushed beyond official boundaries of settlement, claiming vast runs of land, and became the colony's most powerful economic and political class. The tension between squatters and the colonial government over land rights, the reliance on convict and cheap labour, and the environmental transformation of the landscape through sheep grazing reshaped the continent. Understanding the squatocracy is essential for grasping the land ownership patterns, class structures, and frontier violence that defined mid-century Australia.
