# Bushrangers & Colonial Identity (1850s-1880s)

From Ben Hall to Ned Kelly, bushrangers occupied an outsized place in the colonial imagination — celebrated by some as rebels against an unjust system, condemned by others as common criminals. Kelly's 1879 Jerilderie Letter, one of the most remarkable primary sources in Australian history, articulates a class-conscious grievance against police corruption and land inequality. The bushranger phenomenon and its mythologisation in ballads, newspapers, and later literature crystallised a distinctly Australian identity rooted in anti-authoritarianism, mateship, and suspicion of established power.
