# Toward Federation (1890s)

By the 1890s, six separate colonies with different railway gauges, tariff regimes, and immigration laws faced a shared recognition that continental unity offered strategic and economic advantages. The federation movement — driven by figures like Henry Parkes, Edmund Barton, and Alfred Deakin — produced constitutional conventions, popular referendums, and intense debate over what kind of nation Australia would become. The convergence of labour politics, racial anxieties, colonial identity, and Indigenous dispossession into a single constitutional moment makes Federation the culmination of every thread in this course. The convention debates and referendum pamphlets reveal exactly what was — and was not — on the table.
