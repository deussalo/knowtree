# Free Settlement & Assisted Immigration (1820s-1850s)

As the colonies matured, free settlers increasingly outnumbered convicts. Assisted immigration schemes — funded by colonial land sales following Edward Gibbon Wakefield's theories of "systematic colonisation" — brought tens of thousands of British and Irish families to Australia. The founding of South Australia in 1836 as a purely free colony, the end of convict transportation to New South Wales in 1840, and the demographic shift these changes produced transformed the colonies from penal outposts into societies that increasingly demanded self-governance.
