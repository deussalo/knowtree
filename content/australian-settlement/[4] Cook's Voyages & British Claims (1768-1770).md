# Cook's Voyages & British Claims (1768-1770)

Lieutenant James Cook's 1770 charting of the east coast aboard HMS Endeavour transformed Australia from a vaguely known landmass into a specific British territorial claim. His instructions from the Admiralty, his interactions with Aboriginal people at Botany Bay and along the coast, and his declaration of possession at Possession Island set the legal and political stage for colonisation eighteen years later. The primary sources from this voyage — Cook's journal, Joseph Banks's diary, and Sydney Parkinson's sketches — reveal the assumptions Europeans carried with them.
