# Chinese Immigration & Racial Policy (1850s-1880s)

The gold rush brought tens of thousands of Chinese migrants to the Australian colonies, making them the largest non-European group in the population. Their presence triggered violent responses — the Lambing Flat riots of 1860-61, restrictive entry taxes, and residence regulations — that laid the groundwork for the White Australia policy. Examining colonial legislation, newspaper editorials, Chinese community petitions, and testimony from parliamentary inquiries reveals how racial exclusion became embedded in Australian law and identity well before Federation formalised it nationally.
