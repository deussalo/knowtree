# Eureka Stockade & Democratic Reform (1854)

On 3 December 1854, soldiers and police stormed a makeshift stockade of protesting miners at the Eureka lead in Ballarat. The battle lasted minutes, but its political aftermath reshaped Australian governance. The miners' grievances — unjust licence fees, lack of representation, heavy-handed policing — and the jury's refusal to convict the rebel leaders accelerated democratic reforms including expanded suffrage, the secret ballot, and elected legislative councils. The Eureka flag, Peter Lalor's speeches, and the Royal Commission report that followed are primary sources that illuminate how armed rebellion became a founding myth of Australian democracy.
