# Colonial Self-Governance (1850s-1860s)

The Australian Colonies Government Act of 1850 and the constitutions that followed gave New South Wales, Victoria, South Australia, and Tasmania responsible government with elected parliaments. These colonies became laboratories for democratic innovation: Victoria and South Australia introduced the secret ballot (the "Australian ballot"), South Australia granted women's suffrage in 1894, and debates over land reform, education, and tariffs played out in newly formed parliaments. Reading the constitutional debates and early Hansard records reveals how colonists negotiated self-rule while remaining within the British Empire.
