# Convict System & Early Colonial Life (1788-1810s)

Transportation of convicts to New South Wales was the engine that drove the colony's first three decades. The assignment system, the role of the New South Wales Corps (the "Rum Corps"), Governor Macquarie's emancipist policies, and the daily realities of convict labour shaped a society unlike any other in the British Empire. Examining convict records, court transcripts, and Macquarie's dispatches reveals how punishment, survival, and opportunity coexisted in a settlement that was simultaneously a prison and the seed of a new society.
