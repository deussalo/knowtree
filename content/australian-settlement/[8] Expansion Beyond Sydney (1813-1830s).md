# Expansion Beyond Sydney (1813-1830s)

The 1813 crossing of the Blue Mountains by Blaxland, Wentworth, and Lawson opened the interior to pastoral settlement and triggered a wave of expansion. New penal settlements at Van Diemen's Land, Moreton Bay, and the Swan River, plus free settlements in South Australia, transformed Australia from a single coastal outpost into a continent being carved into separate colonies. The speed and pattern of this expansion — driven by land hunger, wool profits, and imperial strategy — set the stage for the pastoral economy and the intensification of conflict with Indigenous peoples.
