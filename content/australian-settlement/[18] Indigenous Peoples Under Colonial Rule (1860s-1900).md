# Indigenous Peoples Under Colonial Rule (1860s-1900)

After the frontier wars, colonial governments shifted from open conflict to administrative control. Protection boards, mission stations, and reserves restricted Aboriginal movement, separated families, controlled wages, and suppressed languages and cultural practices. The Aboriginal Protection Acts passed across the colonies from the 1860s onward created a legal framework of wardship that would persist well into the twentieth century. Board minutes, missionary reports, and the rare surviving petitions and letters from Aboriginal people themselves document both the apparatus of control and the persistence of resistance within it.
